# BIMED Welcome to Ireland Pack

Welcome to Ireland and to BIMED Healthcare. Moving country for work is a major practical change. This pack provides practical orientation for your first days and weeks.

## Before you travel

Keep your key documents together and accessible during travel:

- Passport and travel documents.
- Employment contract or appointment documentation.
- Immigration or employment-permission documentation relevant to your situation.
- BIMED contact information.
- Accommodation confirmation, where arranged.
- Qualification and registration evidence relevant to your role.
- Emergency contact details.

Keep original documents and secure copies separately.

## Arrival plan

Use the interactive arrival form on this page to share your expected arrival date, airport or point of arrival, BIMED contact, accommodation arrangements, first reporting location, and first shift or induction date.

Confirm your arrival arrangements with BIMED before travelling. Do not treat an informal message or provisional date as a confirmed employment start date.

## Your first 48 hours

Priorities are safety, accommodation, communication, and attendance at the agreed induction activities.

- Confirm safe arrival with BIMED's nominated contact.
- Confirm where you are staying and how to reach the workplace.
- Keep your phone charged and your important documents secure.
- Confirm the time and location of your first BIMED meeting or induction.
- Ask questions early if transport, accommodation, or documentation problems may affect attendance.

## Practical settling-in checklist

During your first days, you may need to arrange or confirm matters such as:

- Local mobile and communication arrangements.
- Suitable bank account arrangements for receiving salary.
- Relevant identity, tax, social-insurance, or public-service registration required for your circumstances.
- Local transport route to your normal workplace.
- GP or healthcare arrangements for your personal needs.
- Accommodation and utilities.
- Emergency contacts.

BIMED can signpost employees to official services but does not make personal tax, immigration, housing, or financial decisions for employees.

## Getting around

Plan your route to work before your first shift. Allow additional time during the first week while you learn the local transport network. Keep your supervisor informed promptly if an unavoidable delay may affect attendance.

## Banking and pay

BIMED payroll will confirm the information required to pay your wages. Use only the secure method provided by BIMED when submitting bank and payroll details. Never send banking passwords, PINs, or one-time security codes to BIMED staff.

## Staying safe

In an emergency, use the appropriate Irish emergency service route. For workplace concerns, contact your BIMED supervisor or the designated safeguarding or management contact using the details supplied in your onboarding information.

## Workplace culture

BIMED expects respectful communication, punctuality, professional boundaries, confidentiality, accurate documentation, and a person-centred approach. Treat colleagues, service users, families, and professionals with dignity.

## Your first week at BIMED

Your first week may include:

- Organisational induction.
- Service-specific orientation.
- Introduction to policies and escalation routes.
- Mandatory training.
- Rota and attendance setup.
- Safeguarding and confidentiality orientation.
- Role-specific competency checks.
- Supervisor check-in.

You must not undertake duties for which you have not been trained, authorised, or assessed as competent.

## Your first month

By the end of the first month, you should understand your normal work location, rota, reporting line, key policies, incident and safeguarding reporting routes, attendance expectations, and outstanding compliance requirements.

## Who to contact

Use the contact details supplied in your private BIMED onboarding information for your line manager, recruitment, payroll, and emergency or safeguarding routes.

## Important

This pack contains practical orientation information. Official immigration, employment-permit, tax, housing, healthcare, and public-service requirements can change. Use relevant Irish government or professional-authority information and follow current BIMED instructions.
